================================================================================
SEASONAL ENERGY MANAGEMENT
Season-by-Season Actions to Reduce Energy Use and Costs
================================================================================
Category:  Energy Efficiency / Operations
Audience:  Homeowners, renters, small-building managers
Version:   October 2026

NOTE: Recommendations are general. Climate, building type, equipment and local
rates affect results. Seasons below follow the northern hemisphere; swap the
months for the southern hemisphere. Work on heating equipment, gas appliances,
roofs and electrical systems should be done by qualified professionals.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PRINCIPLES OF SEASONAL ENERGY MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Energy use follows weather. Compare bills using heating and cooling
    degree days, not just calendar months.
  - Prepare before the season starts. Maintenance and weatherization done
    ahead of time prevent expensive emergency repairs and high bills.
  - Use free resources: sun, breezes and shade.
  - Adjust habits as well as equipment. Many seasonal savings are free.
  - Review what worked at the end of each season and record it for next year.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. QUICK REFERENCE SETPOINTS AND SETTINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Setting                          Winter                  Summer
  -------------------------------  ----------------------  --------------------
  Thermostat (home, awake)         about 68 F (20 C)       about 78 F (26 C)
  Thermostat (asleep or away)      lower 7-10 F (4-6 C)    raise a few degrees
  Water heater                     about 120 F (49 C)      about 120 F (49 C)
  Ceiling fan direction            low speed, reversed     airflow downward
                                   (pushes warm air down)  (cooling breeze)
  Window coverings                 open on sunny days,     closed on sunny
                                   closed at night         windows by day
  Indoor humidity target           30-40% (avoid           40-50% (use
                                   condensation)           dehumidification)

Adjust for health needs, age, pets and comfort. Do not set temperatures that
create health risks.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WINTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Before winter (fall prep)
  [ ] Service the heating system; check combustion safety and carbon monoxide
      alarms
  [ ] Replace the furnace filter and stock spares
  [ ] Seal windows, doors, attic hatches and penetrations
  [ ] Add weatherstripping and door sweeps
  [ ] Check attic and crawl-space insulation
  [ ] Clean gutters and check drainage to prevent ice damage
  [ ] Drain and disconnect outdoor hoses; insulate exposed pipes
  [ ] Reverse ceiling fans to low speed
  [ ] Check fireplace damper and chimney
  [ ] Test smart thermostat schedules and backup batteries

3.2 During winter
  - Open curtains on sunny south-facing windows during the day. Close them at
    night to reduce heat loss.
  - Use setbacks while asleep or away. For heat pumps, use gradual recovery
    to avoid backup heating.
  - Keep vents and radiators clear of furniture and drapes.
  - Close fireplace dampers when not in use. An open damper acts like an open
    window.
  - Use bathroom and kitchen exhaust fans only as long as needed, because
    they remove heated air.
  - Lower water heater temperature if above 120 F and insulate exposed hot
    water pipes.
  - Wash clothes in cold or warm water; run full loads.
  - Let the oven's residual heat warm the kitchen after cooking if safe.
  - Check for ice dams, drafts and frost patterns, which can indicate leaks or
    insulation problems.
  - Use space heaters only for brief spot heating, following safety rules.
    Electric resistance heaters are expensive to run for whole-home heating.

3.3 Cold snaps and extreme weather
  - Reduce nonessential loads during peak demand periods.
  - Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during extreme cold to
    protect pipes, and let faucets drip when freezing is expected.
  - Keep a minimum safe indoor temperature when away to prevent pipe damage.
  - Know where your water shutoff is.
  - Have a plan for outages, such as backup heat, blankets, and charged
    devices. Never use generators, grills or camp stoves indoors.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SPRING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Spring is a shoulder season with mild weather. Heating and cooling can often
be off for weeks.

  [ ] Switch off or reduce heating; use natural ventilation on mild days
  [ ] Schedule air conditioner or heat pump maintenance before cooling season
  [ ] Clean or replace filters; clean indoor and outdoor coils
  [ ] Check outdoor unit clearance; remove debris and winter covers properly
  [ ] Inspect ducts, vents and condensate drain
  [ ] Reverse ceiling fans for summer direction
  [ ] Inspect roof, attic ventilation and insulation after winter
  [ ] Clean refrigerator coils and check door seals
  [ ] Clean dryer vent and lint trap
  [ ] Open windows overnight to cool the home when outdoor temperatures drop,
      and close them in the morning if the day will be hot
  [ ] Install or adjust exterior shading, awnings and window films
  [ ] Check weatherstripping and caulking before summer heat
  [ ] Review your last winter's usage and set savings goals for the year
  [ ] Test smart thermostat and update schedules for longer daylight

Spring is also a good time to inspect for moisture and mold, since
temperature swings and rain affect humidity.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SUMMER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Reduce cooling load
  - Close blinds, curtains or shades on sunny windows during the day.
    Exterior shading is more effective than interior coverings.
  - Run ceiling fans when you are in the room. Turn them off when you leave.
  - Avoid using the oven during the hottest hours. Use the microwave, slow
    cooker, air fryer, or outdoor grill (safely away from the building).
  - Run dishwashers and washers in the evening or at night, and air-dry when
    practical.
  - Run the dryer less during peak hours, or use a clothesline.
  - Replace incandescent bulbs, which give off heat, with LEDs.
  - Close doors to unused rooms in zoned or room-based cooling.
  - Reduce the heat from electronics by turning off equipment you are not
    using.

5.2 Run the air conditioner efficiently
  - Set the thermostat as high as comfortable, such as 78 F (26 C) when home.
    Each degree higher saves energy. Avoid dropping the setpoint far below
    the target to "cool faster."
  - Raise the setpoint when no one is home, but avoid extreme settings in
    humid climates.
  - Keep filters clean and supply vents unblocked.
  - Keep the outdoor unit shaded if possible, but do not block airflow.
  - Use pre-cooling in the morning or before peak-rate hours on time-of-use
    plans.
  - Consider "night flushing" in dry or mild-night climates: open windows
    overnight and close them in the morning.

5.3 Hot water and other loads
  - Take shorter showers and fix leaking taps.
  - Run pool pumps for the minimum hours needed, ideally off-peak.
  - Turn off dehumidifiers when not needed, and use the humidity control
    on smart models.

5.4 Heat waves
  - Pre-cool the home early, then reduce load during the peak afternoon.
  - Use fans and cooling spaces in the home. Check on vulnerable people.
  - Avoid heat-generating tasks during the hottest hours.
  - Follow utility alerts and local guidance on conservation or cooling
    centers.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. FALL (AUTUMN)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fall is the best time to prepare for winter and to take advantage of mild
weather.

  [ ] Turn off cooling and use fresh air on mild days
  [ ] Schedule heating system tune-up before the first cold weeks
  [ ] Replace filters; stock spares for winter
  [ ] Seal drafts, replace weatherstripping, and add door sweeps
  [ ] Remove window air conditioners or cover and seal them
  [ ] Cover or protect outdoor air conditioner units if the manufacturer
      recommends it
  [ ] Clean gutters; drain and store hoses; protect outdoor faucets
  [ ] Check smoke and carbon monoxide detectors
  [ ] Reverse ceiling fans to the winter setting (low speed)
  [ ] Open curtains on south-facing windows by day; add insulating curtains
      for night
  [ ] Check attic insulation and ventilation, and add more if needed
  [ ] Schedule chimney inspection if you use a fireplace or wood stove
  [ ] Update smart thermostat schedule for shorter days
  [ ] Consider an energy audit while contractor schedules are open


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. HOLIDAYS, TRAVEL AND OCCASIONAL USE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Holiday lighting and decorations
  - Use LED holiday lights, which use far less energy than incandescent
    strings.
  - Put lights on timers or smart plugs so they run only when useful.

Hosting
  - Extra guests and cooking raise indoor temperatures. Reduce heating before
    guests arrive, and open the oven door safely only when cooking is done.
  - Avoid heating unused rooms.

Vacation and extended absences
  - Set the thermostat to a protective setpoint (not off) in extreme weather.
  - Turn the water heater to vacation mode or lower the temperature, and
    turn off the water supply if leaving for a long time.
  - Unplug nonessential electronics or use smart power strips.
  - Empty and prop open a secondary refrigerator, or turn it off if it will be
    unused.
  - Use timers sparingly for security. LED lights on timers cost little.
  - Ask someone to check the home during extreme weather.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. REGIONAL VARIATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hot-humid climates
    - Focus on moisture control and efficient cooling. Avoid over-cooling.
      Use variable-speed AC, dehumidification and bath/kitchen exhaust.
    - Seal air leaks to keep out humid air.

  Hot-dry climates
    - Shade and reflective roofs reduce cooling load. Night ventilation
      works well. Evaporative coolers can use much less energy than
      compressor-based cooling, but they add moisture and require water.

  Cold climates
    - Priorities are air sealing, insulation and efficient heating. Consider
      cold-climate heat pumps. Protect pipes and monitor ice dams.

  Mixed climates
    - Focus on both heating and cooling load, and use shoulder seasons to
      shut off HVAC and ventilate naturally.

  Marine or mild climates
    - Heating may be modest; high-efficiency heat pumps provide year-round
      comfort. Manage moisture and ventilation.

9. YEAR-ROUND CALENDAR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Month           Key actions
  --------------  -------------------------------------------------------------
  January         Review holiday energy bills; check for drafts and ice dams
  February        Book spring HVAC service; plan efficiency projects
  March           Switch fan direction; service cooling system
  April           Clean coils and vents; check shading; ventilate naturally
  May             Set summer thermostat schedule; add window shading
  June            Check filters monthly; set pool pump schedule
  July            Monitor peak-hour use; pre-cool; avoid oven use
  August          Review cooling bill vs weather; fix issues found
  September       Book heating tune-up; begin weatherizing
  October         Seal drafts; replace filters; reverse fans; clean gutters
  November        Test heating; set winter schedule; insulate pipes
  December        Use LED lights and timers; check holiday and guest loads

Adjust to your local climate and the start of your heating and cooling
seasons.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TRACKING AND IMPROVING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Record monthly electricity and fuel use alongside average temperature.
  - Compare year-over-year using degree days. A mild winter will hide savings;
    a harsh one will hide improvements.
  - Note which actions were taken each season and the results.
  - Set simple goals, such as lowering winter heating use by 10 percent
    compared with the weather-adjusted baseline.
  - Use utility tools, smart thermostats and home energy monitors to see
    patterns.
  - Revisit the plan each quarter.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. COMMON SEASONAL MISTAKES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Waiting for the first extreme weather day to service equipment
  - Leaving ceiling fans in the wrong direction or running in empty rooms
  - Closing too many vents, which can increase duct leakage and strain
    the system
  - Setting the thermostat extremely high or low to speed up heating or cooling
  - Turning the heating system completely off in freezing weather while away
  - Forgetting to remove window air conditioners or seal gaps
  - Leaving outdoor hoses connected in freezing weather
  - Ignoring humidity, which can cause mold in summer and condensation in
    winter
